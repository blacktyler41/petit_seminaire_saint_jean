DATE: 2026-11-01
TITLE:“As he who called you is holy, be holy yourselves in all your conduct; for it is written: ‘You shall be holy, for I am holy.’” (1 Peter 1:15–16)-  Solemnity of All Saints 
FIRST READING: Revelation 7:2-4, 9-14
PSALM: Psalm 24:1bc-2, 3-4ab, 5-6
SECOND READING: 1 John 3:1-3
GOSPEL: Matthew 5:1-12a
MEDITATION:
Dear brothers and sisters in Christ,
 
As we celebrate the Solemnity of All Saints today, allow me to begin this meditation with these words of exhortation from the Apostle Peter, who reminds us that holiness is the ultimate goal of the entire Christian life.
 
The saints whom we commemorate today were men and women of their own time. Like us, they experienced the joys and trials of human existence; like us, they believed in God and walked in faith. However, they left us a precious testimony through their lives, which remains a light and an encouragement on our journey towards God.
 
They form that vast and countless multitude presented to us in today's first reading. They now enjoy the fullness of happiness because they live in God's presence and are filled with His blessings. Indeed, the supreme joy of the saints consists in the beatific vision of God, in that eternal contemplation of the One who is the source of all love and happiness. Conversely, the greatest suffering of the damned consists precisely in being deprived forever of God's presence.
 
But we, dear brothers and sisters, are filled with firm hope: the hope that one day we shall behold the face of God and share in eternal happiness with all the saints who have gone before us. Saint John reminds us of this in the second reading: we are already children of God, although what we shall become has not yet been fully revealed. Nevertheless, we know that when Christ appears, we shall be like Him, because we shall see Him as He really is.
 
In the light of this hope, and in response to the invitation of the eight Beatitudes proclaimed in today's Gospel, let us strive, dear brothers and sisters, to make the Gospel the true light of our lives. Holiness is not an ideal reserved for a few extraordinary people; it is a vocation addressed to each and every one of us. It is built day by day through our choices, our words, our attitudes and, above all, through the way we love God and our neighbour.
 
May our actions, therefore, reflect the charity of Christ, so that at the end of our earthly pilgrimage, the Lord may address these words of promise to us: “Come, you who are blessed by my Father; inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry and you gave me food; I was thirsty and you gave me drink; I was naked and you clothed me…” (Matthew 25:34–36).
 
May our patron saints intercede for us, accompany us through their prayers, and obtain for us the grace to follow Christ faithfully, until the day when we shall be united with them in the glory of the Kingdom of Heaven.
 
A blessed and holy Feast of All Saints to everyone!
